Challenge description:"After my time in space, I prepared for reentry. I remembered my friend who crashed after his space voyage, and for a moment, I thought I might face the same fate. Luckily, I had a smooth landing and was found by two civilians in the area. Soon after, I was sent congratulations from a newspaper correspondent.

Which newspaper did the correspondent write for?"

The "friend who crashed" is a pretty specific detail this isn't a near miss it's someone who actually died in a space accident that immediately narrowed things down historically there haven't been that many fatal spaceflight accidents, and even fewer where a close friend of the survivor was involved.
The "two civilians" detail also felt very specific not a recovery team, not military personnel just two ordinary people who happened to be in the area.

The combination of these clues pointed strongly to Yuri Gagarin
here's why
"My friend who crashed after his space voyage" Gagarin's close friend and fellow cosmonaut Vladimir Komarov was killed on April 24, 1967, when Soyuz 1 crash-landed after its parachutes failed to deploy. Gagarin had actually accompanied Komarov to the rocket before launch and was deeply affected by his death. This is a very well-documented friendship.
"Smooth landing, found by two civilians" After ejecting from Vostok 1 and parachuting down near the village of Smelovka on April 12, 1961, Gagarin was found on the ground by a local farmer and his daughter two civilians. Gagarin himself recalled telling them: "Don't be afraid, I am a Soviet citizen like you, who has descended from space."
"Reentry... thought I might face the same fate" Gagarin's reentry on Vostok 1 actually did have a serious malfunction. The equipment module failed to separate properly, causing the capsule to spin wildly during descent. He survived, but it was genuinely tense.

now finding the newspaper
I searched for documentation of what happened immediately after Gagarin's landing, the key source turned out to be Gagarin's own declassified top secret report to the state commission, given on April 13, 1961  In it, Gagarin describes exactly who greeted him after landing, in his own words:
"Then there were congratulations from the correspondent of Pravda, the correspondent of Izvestiya, and Comrade Il'ichev..."
The footnotes in the document identify the Izvestiya correspondent as Georgiy Nikolayevich Ostroumov (1919–2001) notably, Ostroumov also conducted a full interview with Gagarin that was published in Izvestiya on April 14, 1961 the day after the flight making him the correspondent most directly associated with congratulating Gagarin and covering the story.
